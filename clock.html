How time zones work

The world is divided into time zones defined by their offset from Coordinated Universal Time, written as UTC. A zone at UTC+05:30, such as Sri Lanka and India, is five and a half hours ahead of UTC; a zone at UTC−05:00, such as New York in winter, is five hours behind. The difference between any two places is simply the difference between their offsets.

Most offsets are whole hours, but not all. Sri Lanka, India and Nepal use half-hour or quarter-hour offsets, which is exactly the kind of detail that makes manual conversion error-prone. This page reads each city's offset directly from your device's time zone database, so half-hour zones and unusual offsets are handled correctly without you having to think about them.

Daylight saving time

Many countries shift their clocks forward by an hour in spring and back again in the fall, a practice known as daylight saving time. This is the single biggest source of confusion in international scheduling, because the gap between two cities changes on dates that differ from country to country. Some countries, including Sri Lanka, India, Japan and most of Africa, do not observe it at all.

The clocks on this page adjust for daylight saving automatically, because they use each region's official rules rather than a fixed offset. A city that has just moved its clocks will show the corrected time straight away, so you are never left calculating from an out-of-date assumption.

Converting between US time zones

Because the four mainland zones sit exactly one hour apart, conversions between them are simple once you know the direction. Eastern is one hour ahead of Central, two ahead of Mountain and three ahead of Pacific. So 3:00 PM in New York is 2:00 PM in Chicago, 1:00 PM in Denver and 12:00 PM in Los Angeles.

When does daylight saving time change in the US?

In the United States, daylight saving time begins on the second Sunday in March, when clocks move forward one hour at 2:00 AM local time, and ends on the first Sunday in November, when clocks move back one hour at 2:00 AM. The common reminder is "spring forward, fall back": you lose an hour of sleep in March and gain one in November.

Not every part of the country takes part. Hawaii and most of Arizona stay on standard time all year, though the Navajo Nation within Arizona does observe the change. Puerto Rico, the US Virgin Islands, Guam and American Samoa also remain on standard time year-round. This is why Phoenix is on the same clock as Denver in summer but the same clock as Los Angeles in winter, a quirk that catches out a lot of people scheduling calls.

The clocks on this page follow each region's official rules automatically, so they are already correct on the morning after a change without you needing to do anything.

Scheduling meetings across time zones

When you are arranging a call between countries, a few habits prevent almost all of the usual mistakes. Always state the time zone explicitly rather than saying "3 o'clock" and hoping. Saying "3:00 PM Colombo time (UTC+05:30)" leaves no room for error. Confirm the date as well as the time, since a late-evening meeting in Asia often falls on the previous calendar day in the Americas, something the world clock above makes immediately obvious.

It also helps to check the gap close to the meeting date rather than weeks ahead, because daylight saving changes can quietly move the difference by an hour in between. Adding the cities you work with to this page is the quickest way to keep that visible.

Frequently asked questions

What is the time difference between EST and PST?

Three hours. Pacific time is always three hours behind Eastern time, so 3:00 PM in New York is 12:00 PM in Los Angeles. The gap stays the same year-round because both zones change for daylight saving on the same dates.

Does Arizona change its clocks?

Most of Arizona does not, so it stays on Mountain Standard Time all year. That puts Phoenix on the same clock as Denver in winter and the same clock as Los Angeles in summer. The Navajo Nation within Arizona is the exception and does observe daylight saving.

Is the time on this page accurate?

The clock reads the time from your own device, so it is exactly as accurate as your device's clock. Most computers and phones synchronize automatically with internet time servers and stay accurate to well under a second.

How do I add a city that is not shown?

Press Add City, select the country, choose the time zone from the list, and edit the title if you want a different label. The new clock appears immediately and is saved for your next visit.

How do I remove a city I added?

Hover over the card you added and click the × in its top-right corner. The ten default cities cannot be removed.

Does the world clock adjust for daylight saving time?

Yes, automatically. Each city uses its region's official daylight saving rules rather than a fixed offset, so the displayed time is correct year-round.

Why does another city show a different date?

That is normal. Time zones far apart can be on opposite sides of midnight, so a city may legitimately be a calendar day ahead of or behind you. The date under each clock makes this clear.
