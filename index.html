Online Alarm Clock

ClockHub is a free online alarm clock that runs entirely in your web browser. There is nothing to download, nothing to install and no account to create. You simply choose the hour and minute you want to be woken up, pick an alarm sound, and press Set Alarm. When that moment arrives, your chosen sound plays and a full-screen wake-up message appears so the alarm is impossible to miss, even if you have wandered off to another browser tab.

The clock at the top of this page shows the current time in your own time zone, detected automatically from your device. That means an alarm set for 7:00 AM rings at 7:00 AM where you are, whether you are in Colombo, London or New York, so there is no time zone math to worry about and no settings to configure before you start.

How to set an alarm online

Setting an alarm takes about five seconds. The three steps below cover everything you need to know.

  1. Choose the time. Use the Hour, Minute and AM/PM selectors, or simply tap one of the quick preset buttons such as 6:00 AM or 7:30 AM if you want a common wake-up time.
  2. Pick and preview a sound. Select Classic Bell, Digital Beep or Gentle Chime, then press Test to hear it. Testing also unlocks audio playback in your browser, which is worth doing the first time you use the page.
  3. Press Set Alarm. The status line confirms the time you have chosen and the alarm becomes active. Leave the tab open and you are done.

If you change your mind, press Cancel Alarm at any point before it rings. Once the alarm is sounding, the same button, and the button in the wake-up overlay, stops it immediately.

Choosing the right alarm sound

Different situations call for different sounds, and the three options here were chosen to cover the most common ones.

Classic Bell is a repeating bell tone in the tradition of a mechanical alarm clock. It is the most attention-grabbing of the three and the best choice for waking up from deep sleep. Digital Beep is a sharp, high-pitched electronic tone that cuts through background noise well, which makes it useful in a noisy kitchen, workshop or office. Gentle Chime is a soft ascending three-note melody designed to bring you around gradually rather than with a jolt, a good fit for short naps, meditation sessions and quiet shared spaces such as libraries or open-plan offices.

All three sounds are generated by your browser using the Web Audio API rather than downloaded as audio files. That keeps the page extremely light and means the alarm still works even on a slow or unreliable internet connection, since nothing needs to be fetched when the alarm fires.

Why use an online alarm clock?

A browser-based alarm is often more convenient than reaching for a phone. If you are already working at a computer, setting an alarm in a tab is faster than unlocking your phone, opening the clock app and tapping through a time picker. It is also the obvious answer when your phone is charging in another room, has run out of battery, or is set to silent for a meeting.

Common uses include waking up from a power nap without oversleeping, reminding yourself when a meeting or class starts, timing how long you spend on a task, keeping an eye on food in the oven, remembering to take medication at a fixed time each day, and making sure you leave the house at the right moment. Students often use an online alarm while studying for exams, and shift workers use one on shared or work computers where installing software is not permitted.

If you need a countdown rather than a fixed clock time, meaning "wake me in twenty minutes" rather than "wake me at 3 o'clock", then the online countdown timer is the better tool for the job. To measure how long something takes rather than being alerted at a set point, use the stopwatch with lap times.

Will the alarm still ring if…

These are the three situations people most often worry about, and the honest answers.

…the tab is in the background? Yes. The alarm checks the time continuously and will sound even when you are working in another tab or another application. The browser tab title also flashes so you can spot which tab is ringing.

…my screen is off or the computer is asleep? No. When a computer sleeps it stops running the page entirely, so the alarm cannot fire. If you are relying on the alarm to wake you, change your power settings so the machine does not sleep, or keep it plugged in with sleep disabled.

…my sound is muted? No. The alarm plays through your normal system audio, so check that both your device volume and your browser tab are not muted. Pressing Test before you rely on the alarm is the simplest way to confirm that sound is working.

Tips for waking up on time

An alarm clock only solves half the problem. The other half is what you do around it. Placing your device far enough away that you have to stand up to stop the alarm makes going back to sleep considerably less likely. Setting a single alarm rather than a chain of snoozes tends to leave people feeling more alert, because repeatedly falling back into light sleep fragments the final part of your sleep cycle.

Where possible, aim for a consistent wake-up time across the week, including weekends; a stable schedule makes the alarm far easier to respond to. And if you are using the alarm for a nap rather than overnight sleep, keeping the nap to around twenty minutes usually avoids the grogginess that comes with waking from deeper sleep stages.

Frequently asked questions

Is this online alarm clock really free?

Yes, completely. There is no account, no subscription, no trial period and no limit on how many times you can use it. Your preferences (theme, chosen sound and recently used alarm times) are stored in your own browser and never sent anywhere.

Do I need to install anything?

No. ClockHub runs entirely in your browser using standard web technology, so it works on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android and iOS without any download or plugin.

Can I set more than one alarm at a time?

One alarm can be active at a time per browser tab. If you need several alarms, open ClockHub in more than one tab and set a different time in each.

Does the alarm work without an internet connection?

Once the page has loaded, yes. The alarm sounds are generated in your browser rather than streamed, so the alarm still fires if your connection drops afterwards.

Why does the alarm sound not play until I click something?

Browsers block audio until the user interacts with the page, which prevents websites from playing sound unexpectedly. Pressing Test or Set Alarm counts as that interaction and unlocks audio for the session.

What time zone does the alarm use?

Your own. The page reads the time zone from your device, so the alarm always rings at the local time you set. You can check which zone was detected just below the clock.
